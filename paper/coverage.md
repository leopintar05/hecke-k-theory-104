# Two auxiliary fields and the signed Hecke tests through degree 104

**Theorem.** Assuming the arithmetic construction in Alpöge’s pinned Step 17, we obtain $K_{4k}(\mathbb Z)=0$ for $1\le k\le26$. Section 1 identifies the Selmer group and obtains the common residual representation from this construction. Coverage for the representations that can witness a nonzero group follows from the three Bernoulli units, compact unitary transfer and the signed determinant argument.

This argument follows Levent Alpöge’s suggestion in Step 17, footnote 6. We use the joint-representation theorem, the Bernoulli reduction and the exclusion of two source types from the Grothendieck–Montpellier Archive’s *KTOWER*, checkpoint 11. ## 1. A single representation and a single coefficient prime

For even $2\le e\le52$ and a prime $\ell\ge2^{31}$, set $D=(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)(-e)$ and $E_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ for $d=3,7$. Define $S_e\subset H^1(\mathbb Q,D)$ by local triviality on all finite inertia groups and on the decomposition groups at $2,\ell$.

**Comparison at the trivial character.** For $N=2$, the trivial character, and the coefficient ring given by the image of $\mathbb Z_\ell$, `vlen_one` identifies `Vlen` with the length of the rational group `ShaU` for the negative cyclotomic twist and the integer $2\ell$. Its coefficient module is $\mathbb Z_\ell\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)$ with that twist, hence $D$. By `len_eq_zero_iff`, zero length means that every continuous cocycle that is a coboundary on each inertia group, and on each decomposition group above a divisor of $2\ell$, is a global coboundary. The coboundary witness can be chosen separately at each local subgroup. These are exactly the restrictions defining $S_e$. Thus $\mathrm{Vlen}=0\iff S_e=0$. Both sides are defined over $\mathbb Q$, so the conclusion applies to both auxiliary fields. [Trivial-character identity](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SmallClassNumbers/ClassNumberThree.lean#L13-L30) · [Coefficient module, local conditions and zero criterion](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SmallClassNumbers/LenEqZeroIff.lean#L9-L63)

**Passage from $K_{2e}$ to a strict class.** If $K_{2e}(\mathbb Z)/\ell\ne0$, the cyclotomic comparison gives a nonzero $\omega^{-e}$-component of $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell))/\ell$. An equivariant quotient onto $D[\ell]$ gives a nonzero unramified class over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$. The unique prime above $\ell$ is principal, so its Frobenius in that quotient is trivial. The extension degree $\ell-1$ is prime to $\ell$, giving descent by inflation–restriction and preserving local triviality at $\ell$. At each other finite prime the cyclotomic extension is unramified, so the descended class is unramified there. Since $0<e<\ell-1$, the mod-$\ell$ character $\omega^{-e}$ is nontrivial and $D^{G_{\mathbb Q}}=0$. The exact sequence for multiplication by $\ell$ on $D$ therefore injects the descended class into $H^1(\mathbb Q,D)$. At 2 its unramified local class lies in $D/(2^{-e}-1)D=0$, since the nonzero scalar acts surjectively on $D$. This proves $K_{2e}(\mathbb Z)/\ell\ne0\Rightarrow S_e\ne0\Rightarrow\mathrm{Vlen}>0$. [Cyclotomic comparison, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.00108v3#S5)

Now take $e=52$ for the degree-104 argument. Fix $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, a coefficient identification $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, $N=2$, the trivial character, and $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z_\ell$ of ramification index one. The character has order one, trivial square and the required parity since $e$ is even. Each $E_d$ is imaginary quadratic of discriminant $-d$ and class number one by the Minkowski bound $2\sqrt d/\pi<2$. Hence $\ell\nmid d h(E_d)$ and $(d,N)=1$. Ramification at $d$ gives $E_d\not\subset\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$, the kernel condition for this character. Finally $\ell$ is odd and $0<2e=104<\ell-1$. These are all the `GD` conditions, in both fields. The joint theorem uses nine inputs to the source construction. They are `CohInp`, `LemmaM`, `LemmaM'`, `LemmaP`, `KatoTwoW`, `RigidityFirstCase`, `RigiditySecondCase`, `AdmI_s` and `AutomorphyUnramifiedCoefficientsAlt`. The proof terms are supplied inside `corGK_of` with the hypothesis discharged as in `corGK`. They give a deformation ring and a model before a Galois element is chosen. If there were no points satisfying `TwoPt`, the covering set in `model_upper` at the identity would be empty, forcing finite model length zero. Then `vlen_le` would force `Vlen` to vanish. Thus there is an actual point $f$. Its integral representation has inverse matrices integral as well. For this one $f$, `isPt_push` reduces every matrix on $G_{E_d}$ to the diagonal target. The admissibility proof in `FOneCharU` places it in `Adm3U_s` at level 2 or `Adm2A` at level $2d$. In particular

$$
\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho_f(g)=1+\omega(g)^e+\omega(g)^{-e}
\qquad\text{for every }g\in G_{E_d}.
$$

Here $\omega$ denotes the residual cyclotomic character. The coefficient field of $f$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ with the valuation inherited from $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. Both split directions and every other tested Frobenius use this same coefficient prime. Each auxiliary field can have its own $f$. The closed inputs underlying the pointwise inequality thus supply a common representation in each field. ## 2. The two branches excluded by the units

Write $\eta_d=\chi_{-d}$, $\beta_{n,d}=B_{n,\eta_d}/n$, and $A_d(e)=|\operatorname{num}\beta_{e+1,d}\operatorname{num}\beta_{2e+1,d}|$, with each fraction reduced. For $a=e,2e$, FV.3 gives $B_{1,\eta_d\omega^a}\equiv\beta_{a+1,d}\bmod\ell$, using the Teichmüller lift of $\omega$. The untwisted $B_{1,\eta_d}$ equals $-1/3$ for $d=3$ and $-1$ for $d=7$. At $e=52$ the denominators of the two remaining rational values are $3,9$ for $E_3$ and $1,49$ for $E_7$. [Bernoulli reduction FV.3](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/grothendieck-montpellier-archive/blob/edb183a9af46cb9568e0d3caf2248e98b491e6fb/raw/research_returns/KTOWER_checkpoint_11_20261122T0001Z_unzipped/KTOWER.tex#L7799-L7829)

Let $p_1=43279339111,\ p_2=490440141803,\ p_3=63421406191963401438831238997,\ p_4=|\operatorname{num}\beta_{105,3}|/2162$ and $M=p_1p_2p_3p_4$. The certified factors are prime. Direct rational computation gives

$$
|\operatorname{num}\beta_{53,3}|=2p_1p_2p_3,\qquad
|\operatorname{num}\beta_{105,3}|=2\cdot23\cdot47\,p_4,\qquad
\gcd(A_3(52),A_7(52))=4.
$$

For $\ell\nmid M$ all three ES.5 quantities are units in $E_3$. For each $\ell\mid M$ all three are units in $E_7$. Their roles are distinct. The first gives the class-number condition. The third excludes Type II using the rank-two representation and determinant supplied by an actual source point. The second excludes a self-dual Type I point. At level 2 the allowed finite inertial characters are trivial, since $\mu_2\subset\mathbb Q$. A Type I point is unramified away from $\ell$, has the ordered ordinary inertial characters $(\chi_\ell^e,1,\chi_\ell^{-e})$ above $\ell$, and has the global residual triple above. The finite-coefficient and cuspidal attachment conditions are those verified in the source application of the self-dual exclusion. Under the first unit the essential-self-duality lemma makes any self-duality up to twist genuine. ES.5 therefore leaves only non-essentially-self-dual, cuspidal rank-three points in the selected field. This is precisely the coverage needed for a Selmer witness. ## 3. The level and normalization of the surviving representation

For such a point, twist $\rho_f$ by $\chi_\ell^{-1}$ to use its automorphic attachment. The level witnesses at $N=2$ also work at modulus one since they factor through the action on $\mu_2$. The source’s nonadjacent flag condition is preserved under the twist. Its level theorem then gives a regular algebraic cuspidal conjugate-self-dual $\Pi$ on ${\rm GL}_3/E_d$, spherical at every finite place, with infinitesimal parameter $(e,0,-e)$. Its central character is unramified at finite places and trivial at infinity, hence trivial by class number one. At a geometric Frobenius of norm $Q$ the twist multiplies $\rho_f$ by $Q$, and `algSat` also multiplies the unitary Satake roots by $Q$. Thus $\operatorname{tr}\rho_f$ is their sum at every tested place, including those above 2. Use the definite unitary group of the standard ternary Hermitian form and the full stabilizer of its lattice. It is a pure inner form, quasi-split at every finite place. The infinity characters $(z/\bar z)^e,1,(z/\bar z)^{-e}$ have the positive parity for the untwisted stable embedding. The compact member has highest weight $(e-1,0,1-e)$. Cuspidality makes the global parameter simple and generic. Its reduced component group is trivial, so the generic pure-inner-form multiplicity formula makes any choice of the relevant local members occur. At split places choose the spherical ${\rm GL}_3$ member, and at unramified inert places choose the member fixed by the hyperspecial lattice stabilizer. At the ramified place $d=3$ or $7$, write $L/F=(E_d)_w/\mathbb Q_d$. The unramified parameter of $\Pi_w$ has roots $\{z,1,z^{-1}\}$ by conjugate self-duality and determinant one. They are unitary by Shin’s odd-rank temperedness theorem. On the quasi-split unitary torus $L^\times\times U(1)$ take the unramified character with parameter $z$ on $L^\times$ and the trivial character on $U(1)$. Its stable base change has precisely these three characters. The fixed-parity base-change map on parameters identifies its parameter with the required local one. The tempered packet contains the constituents of this normalized induction. If $B$ is the Borel and $J_d$ the **full** lattice stabilizer, the inducing character and the modulus are trivial on $B\cap J_d$. The induced function supported on $BJ_d$ with value one at the identity is therefore $J_d$-fixed. Exactness of compact invariants gives a constituent of this packet with the required vector, including when roots coincide. This argument works at both ramified primes. The resulting full compact space has one lattice class over $E_3$ and two over $E_7$. ## 4. Why the smaller determinants suffice

Conjugation of $E_d$, together with the swap of the two sets of polynomial variables in the coefficient representation, defines the linear involution $J$. On the standard class it acts by $JP(x,y)=P(y,x)$. It preserves the standard lattice class and hence also the other class over $E_7$. An identification on that class is well-defined on stabilizer invariants. It exchanges the two split Hecke directions and fixes the inert operator. Take an eigenvector $v$ in the compact representation attached to $\Pi$. The vectors $v,Jv$ have distinct characteristic-zero Hecke systems. Indeed $\Pi^c=\Pi^\vee$ and equality with $\Pi$ would give $\rho_f\simeq\rho_f^\vee$ by Frobenius compatibility and Chebotarev. Some good split place distinguishes the systems by strong multiplicity one, since conjugation fixes each unramified inert local representation. Thus $v-Jv\ne0$, and for each tested conjugation-invariant operator it has the same eigenvalue as $v$.

**First field.** At degree 104 the shifted matrices on the negative subspace of dimension 324 are

$$
 X=4^{51}(H+3I)-u_4I,\qquad
 Z=7^{51}(H_w+H_{\bar w})-2u_7I .
$$

On $v-Jv$ both eigenvalues reduce to zero. These operators have integral characteristic polynomials at $\ell$. More generally if $P$ is such a polynomial and $P(s)=0,\ s\equiv0\bmod\lambda$, then $P(0)\equiv0\bmod\lambda$. Applying this to each characteristic polynomial gives $\ell\mid\det X$ and $\ell\mid\det Z$, even if $v\equiv Jv\bmod\lambda$ in a chosen lattice. The archived integers give

$$
\gcd(\det X,\det Z)
=3^{1578}5^{33}11^{31}19^{18}23^6\cdot29\cdot79\,p_1p_2p_3 .
$$

This excludes all $\ell\ge2^{31}$ outside $M$. The fourth prime only needs testing in the second field because it divides $A_3(52)$. [Degree-104 certificates](https://github.com/leopintar05/hecke-k-theory-104/releases/tag/v1.1)

**Second field.** The scaled sum $2^{51}(H_w+H_{\bar w})$ on the two classes has blocks $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\D&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The zero comes from all seven neighbors of the nonstandard class returning to the standard class, in each direction. With $t=2u_2$, its eigenvalue $s$ on $v-Jv=(x,y)$ satisfies $s\equiv t\bmod\lambda$ and $\gcd(t,M)=1$. Consequently $s\ne0,\ x\ne0$, and eliminating $y$ in characteristic zero gives

$$
 (s^2 I-sA-BD)x=0,\qquad
 F(T)=\det\!\left((T^2I-TA-BD)|_{H_0^-}\right),\qquad F(s)=0 .
$$

Here $H_0^-$ is the standard-class negative space of rank $\binom{27}{3}=2925$. The coefficients of $F$ are integral at each tested $\ell$. Indeed the polynomial coefficient lattice modulo contraction has a torsion-free integral model (division by the monic leading term of the contraction gives a monomial basis). The neighbor maps and return paths are integral away from 2 on these lattices, using the two class representatives supported at 2. Taking stabilizer invariants and the odd-prime negative projector preserves local lattices. It follows that $F(t)\equiv F(s)=0\bmod\lambda$. The certificate evaluates $L(t)=t^2I-tA-BD$ at integral incidence pairs in an integral polynomial basis of $H_0^-$, giving $W=V[L(t)]$ in characteristic zero. Thus $\det W=\det V\,F(t)$ and $\det V$ is integral. This forces $\ell\mid\det W$ even if the evaluation matrix is singular after reduction. The stored value gives $\gcd(\det W,M)=1$, excluding all four primes. ## 5. Lower degrees and conclusion

For every even $2\le e\le50$ the same field hypotheses and local proofs apply. The odd part of $\gcd(A_3(e),A_7(e))$ is 1 except at $e=12,18,36,42$, where it is respectively $47,37,37^2,43^2$. Thus every $\ell\ge2^{31}$ again has all three units in $E_3$ when $\ell\nmid A_3(e)$, or in $E_7$ when $\ell\mid A_3(e)$. The archive gives $v_\ell(\gcd(C_2U_2,C_7U_7))=v_\ell(|\operatorname{num}\beta_{e+1,3}|)+3v_\ell(|\operatorname{num}\beta_{2e+1,3}|)$ for $\ell>337$. A stable source forces both full compact determinants $U_2,U_7$ to vanish modulo $\ell$, so the first-field calculation excludes every prime outside $A_3(e)$.

| Degrees | What excludes the remaining large primes |
|---|---|
| $4,8,\ldots,40$ | The existing cyclotomic checks, with one directed $E_7$ compact test each in degrees 32 and 40. Degrees 4 and 8 also have published vanishing. |
| $44,48$ | Directed $E_7$ compact tests at two primes and one prime, respectively. |
| $52,56,60,64,68$ | Directed $E_7$ compact tests at $4,2,4,4,3$ primes, respectively. |
| $72$ | The rank-1,944 directed test has an inverse modulo a product containing every survivor. |
| $76,80,\ldots,100$ | The seven signed tests have $\gcd(t_e,M_e)=\gcd(\det W_e,M_e)=1$, where $t_e=2(2^{2e}+2^e+1)$ and $M_e$ contains all large divisors of $A_3(e)$. |

For odd primes below $2^{31}$, the cyclotomic comparison and the verified Vandiver range give vanishing. The known 2-primary result and finiteness finish the deduction. The sole project-specific hypothesis is the arithmetic construction of the pinned Step 17. Its strict Selmer comparison in these specializations follows from the two source identities in Section 1. The proof uses the construction behind the pointwise inequality to obtain an actual point. The numerical inequality in isolation permits the witness to vary. The arithmetic was checked against the archived determinants and residues, with a new exact calculation of the Bernoulli units.
